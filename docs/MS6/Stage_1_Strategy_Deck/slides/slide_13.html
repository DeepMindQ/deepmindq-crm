Design Pattern #2 & #3 — Progressive Disclosure & Cognitive Load Reduction

LEVEL 1 DECISION LAYER

What & Why

Headline, confidence score, priority badge, timestamp. The user sees intelligence at a glance — enough to decide: do I need to act on this now?

arrow_forward
LEVEL 2 REASONING LAYER

Why Does the System Think This?

Primary reasoning statement, positive and negative confidence factors, impact analysis. Answers the trust question before the user asks it.

arrow_forward
LEVEL 3 EVIDENCE LAYER

Show Me the Proof

Evidence chain with sources, dates, relevance scores, and verification links. Full transparency for users who need to validate.

arrow_forward
LEVEL 4 EXPLORATION

What Else Should I Know?

Related signals, connected insights, adjacent opportunities, and deeper research paths.

psychology

Each level is independently accessible. Users who trust the system stay at L1. Skeptics drill to L3. Researchers explore L4. The experience adapts to the user's trust level — the user is never forced to digest complexity they do not need.

Progressive disclosure is the mechanism that makes Intelligence as Executive Briefing work. Not every user needs the same depth. A VP Sales who trusts the system after weeks of use may never drill past Level 1. A new user or a skeptic will want Level 3 evidence. The key insight: each level is independently accessible and answers a specific cognitive need.
